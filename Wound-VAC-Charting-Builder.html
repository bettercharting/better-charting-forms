Golden rule: Document what you see, what you did, and why a skilled nurse was required. Every NPWT note needs: wound assessment, foam count (in and out), pressure setting, seal status, pump function, patient tolerance, and teaching. Count in, count out — every time.

🩸 Wound Assessment

▼
Measure every visit. Length (head-to-toe) × Width (side-to-side) × Depth (deepest point). Note tissue % and exudate. Track the trend — change or no change, both are significant.

⚙️ NPWT Setup & Settings

▼
Count in, count out — every time. If the count doesn't match, do not leave until resolved. Retained foam = infection risk. Standard pressure: -125 mmHg continuous. Lower (-75) for fragile tissue. Higher (-150 to -200) for heavy drainage.

🔧 Seal & Pump Status

▼
Most common NPWT problem: lost seal. Check tubing connection, kinks, canister seating first. Then check drape edges — run finger around slowly to find air leak. Patch, don't redo. If periwound is moist or sweaty, use benzoin or ostomy paste.

🤕 Pain & Tolerance

▼
Pre-medicate 30 min before if ordered. NPWT dressing changes can be painful, especially first few changes. If pain is recurring, talk to provider about lowering pressure or adding a non-adherent interface layer.

📚 Patient & Caregiver Teaching

▼
Check all that apply:







Teach the DO NOT TOUCH rule before you leave. The #1 reason for between-visit alarms: a caregiver touched the pump or drape. Show them what to do, have them demonstrate back, and leave a written card: pump stays on, keep upright, call if it alarms.

📞 Escalation

▼
When to call: spreading erythema, purulent drainage, foul odor, fever ≥100.4°F, uncontrolled pain, wound stalled or increasing, maceration spreading, foam count mismatch, pump failure with no backup. If you called — document time, who, what was discussed, and orders received.

🏥 Skilled Rationale

▼
Every NPWT note must answer: why does this wound require a skilled nurse? State what the nurse did that the patient/caregiver cannot do safely — NPWT technique, seal management, infection monitoring, foam-count safety, and teaching.

📅 Next Visit Plan

▼
State the plan: when is the next dressing change (per orders), what will you assess, and what complications are you watching for? Instruct patient/caregiver to call if signs develop before next scheduled visit.
